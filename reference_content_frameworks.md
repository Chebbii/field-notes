Living log of outside frameworks worth adapting into planner content or product features. Each entry: plain-language explanation, verified source, concrete application idea. No duplicates — check existing entries before adding.

## New, verified 2026-08-30

### Implementation Intentions ("If-Then Planning")
Psychologist Peter Gollwitzer's research shows that turning a vague goal into a specific "if [situation], then I will [action]" statement makes people far more likely to follow through — a meta-analysis of 94 studies found a medium-to-large effect (d = .65) on goal attainment, and a later 642-study analysis confirmed the effect holds across cognitive, emotional, and behavioral goals. The mechanism: pre-deciding the trigger removes the need for in-the-moment willpower or memory, which is exactly where ADHD executive function struggles.
Source: [If-then planning, Taylor & Francis review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10463283.2020.1808936); [Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement meta-analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/37367696_Implementation_Intentions_and_Goal_Achievement_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Effects_and_Processes)
Product application: Replace open-ended "goals" fields in the planner with a forced if-then micro-prompt template ("If it's 9am and I haven't opened my laptop, then I do a 2-minute desk reset") — one per daily page, tied to the user's stated transition goal.

### Temptation Bundling
Wharton professor Katy Milkman's 2014 randomized field experiment paired an instantly-gratifying "want" (audiobooks/page-turner novels) with a valuable but avoided "should" (gym visits), restricting access to the want to only-while-doing-the-should. Result: 51% more gym visits during the study, with a 2020 follow-up showing elevated workout frequency persisting 17 weeks after the intervention ended.
Source: [Holding the Hunger Games Hostage at the Gym (SSRN)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2183859); [Knowledge at Wharton coverage](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/researchers-used-hunger-games-encourage-healthier-choices/)
Product application: A planner module that has users explicitly pair one dreaded task with one guilty-pleasure item ("only listen to [podcast] while doing [avoided admin task]"), logged as a locked pair rather than two separate to-dos — makes the bundling a structural constraint, not just a suggestion.

## New, verified 2026-09-02

### WOOP / Mental Contrasting with Implementation Intentions (MCII)
NYU psychologist Gabriele Oettingen's four-step protocol — Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan — combines vividly imagining a desired outcome ("mental contrasting") with an if-then plan for the specific obstacle that will get in the way. Her research base (25+ years, including RCTs on weight management, physical activity in stroke survivors, and VA weight-loss programs) found that pure positive visualization alone can backfire — the brain treats the imagined success as already achieved and reduces the effort put toward it — while contrasting that image against a named obstacle, then planning for it, reliably outperforms wishful thinking alone.
Source: [Gabriele Oettingen's Publications, NYU Motivation Lab](https://wp.nyu.edu/motivationlab/publications/gabriele-oettingen/); [What Is Mental Contrasting and How Can We Benefit From It? (Positive Psychology)](https://positivepsychology.com/mental-contrasting/)
Product application: A "reality-check" variant of the planner's goal page that forces four short fields in order — Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan — instead of the more common "just visualize success" prompt seen in most planners; positions the brand against toxic-positivity goal-setting content, which is a natural talking point for the life-transition audience.

### Plan-Making Eliminates Intrusive Thoughts About Unfinished Goals
Building on the classic Zeigarnik effect (unfinished tasks are remembered better and intrude on thought more than finished ones), a 2011 study by E.J. Masicampo and Roy Baumeister (Journal of Personality and Social Psychology) found that writing a specific when-and-how plan for an unfinished goal — without actually completing it — reduced intrusive thoughts and cognitive interference on an unrelated task just as effectively as finishing the goal did. The plan itself, not the completion, is what quiets the mental "open loop."
Source: [Consider It Done! Plan Making Can Eliminate the Cognitive Effects of Unfulfilled Goals, Masicampo & Baumeister 2011 (PDF)](https://users.wfu.edu/masicaej/MasicampoBaumeister2011JPSP.pdf)
Product application: A nightly "brain dump → schedule it" ritual page: any task logged as unfinished at day's end must get a specific day/time slot before the page can be marked closed — directly targeting the racing-thoughts-at-bedtime complaint common in ADHD and life-transition audiences, and distinct from Implementation Intentions above (that entry is about follow-through rate; this one is about relieving rumination even before the task is done).
